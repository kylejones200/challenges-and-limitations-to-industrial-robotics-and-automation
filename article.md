### Challenges and limitations to industrial robotics and automation
#### Industrial robots bring numerous benefits to manufacturing facilities and pave the way for future smart factories. However, there are certain limitations that manufacturers must consider when choosing their robotic equipment.
The need for automated processes is increasing. Fortune Business Insights predicts the industrial robot market will reach \$31.3 billion by 2028. Manufacturers are increasingly aware of robot deployment's potential business and production benefits. However, industrial robots have their drawbacks. Here are some of their most common limitations and suggestions on what manufacturers can do to overcome them.

**Affordability**

Industrial robots usually require a significant initial investment, including additional setup and configuration costs. Manufacturers must also consider future maintenance costs and the need for additional components.

Likewise, the robotics sector constantly evolves, and updated machines always appear on the market. Investing in new robots regularly could prove difficult for some companies, especially smaller ones, which could go bankrupt trying to keep up with industry trends.

However, industrial robots can help manufacturers reduce costs in different areas. They can reduce production costs and increase profits thanks to work optimization. With a clear investment strategy and financial plan, robots are more likely to generate a quick return on investment.

Another smart alternative is to invest in refurbished robots. Used robots are generally half the cost of new ones and remain efficient and operational.

**Security**

Industrial robots have always been considered dangerous in the factory. And with good reason: they are large, bulky pieces of equipment and capable of moving very quickly. Older machines even lack the sensory ability to detect nearby humans, making them prone to dangerous collisions and accidents. For this reason, many manufacturers add cages or dividers to separate the robots from their human co-workers.

More recently, thanks to the introduction of collaborative robots, which are smaller and lighter and are specifically designed to work with humans, safety has become a top priority in industrial automation. More regulatory practices have been implemented for both bulky industrial robots and cobots.

There is little question about progress, even though there is still some work to achieve total production safety. New technologies such as light curtains, laser scanners, and presence detection devices are widely accepted as a method to increase human safety.

Another good practice for manufacturers is to carry out an individual risk assessment of their production line and train workers to react in the event of an accident.

**More difficult to form**

Industrial robots require expert programming and training to perform tasks, so companies must hire experienced technicians and programmers to oversee robot installation. Furthermore, even experienced staff may need to update their skills when new software is developed, or new robots come on the market. If a robot is not programmed correctly, it can malfunction and harm those around it.

However, in recent years a new method of robot training has been implemented: no-code or low-code programming. It allows employees with less coding experience to configure a robot using visual modeling and drag-and-drop user interfaces. Due to the easy-to-use format of low-code and no-code platforms, it is also possible to reprogram robots to perform different tasks by adjusting their arm. While companies once required multiple robots, someone with no technical qualifications can now make a simple adjustment, saving companies time, space, and money.

The low-code and no-code platform trend is rising, with 84% of US, UK, Canadian, and Australian companies implementing low-code development platforms to reduce coding needs.

Industrial robots have been shown to simplify human work, bring a quick return on investment for manufacturers and optimize production. However, they have limitations. They are more challenging to train than humans, require high investment and maintenance costs, and pose safety concerns. Although these are legitimate concerns for manufacturers, they can be addressed with careful planning and new technologies to overcome them.

**Maintenance and Repair**

Maintenance and repair are one of the limitations of industrial robotics and automation. While robotic systems offer many advantages, such as increased productivity and reduced labor costs, they can also be complex and require specialized expertise to maintain and repair.

Robotic systems have many components, including mechanical, electrical, and software. Each component requires regular maintenance to ensure the system operates reliably and efficiently. For example, mechanical components such as motors, gears, and bearings need regular lubrication, inspection, and replacement to prevent wear and damage.

Electrical components such as sensors, controllers, and power supplies can also fail over time due to age, wear, or environmental factors. Maintenance of these components typically involves testing and replacing faulty components and calibrating sensors to ensure accurate readings.

Software is another critical component of robotic systems that requires regular maintenance and updates. Software bugs, compatibility issues, and security vulnerabilities can cause system failures or compromise performance. Regular software updates and maintenance can ensure the system is operating efficiently and securely.

Repairing robotic systems can be a complex and time-consuming process that requires specialized expertise. Identifying the cause of a failure or malfunction can be challenging, as many different components may be involved. Additionally, repairing a system may require specialized tools or equipment that is not readily available or requires specialized training.

#### Limited Fexibility
Flexibility is one of the limitations of industrial robotics and automation. While robotic systems offer many advantages, they can also be limited in adapting to changing conditions or performing tasks requiring complex decision-making.

Robotic systems are typically designed and programmed to perform specific tasks in a specific way. For example, a robot on an assembly line may be programmed to perform the same task repeatedly, such as tightening bolts or welding seams. While this approach can be highly efficient and reliable, it can also limit the system's flexibility and adaptability.

Robots may adapt to changing conditions or perform complex decision-making tasks in many industrial applications. For example, a robot in a warehouse may need to navigate around obstacles or adjust its movements based on changes in the environment. Such tasks can be challenging to program and may require sophisticated algorithms and sensors.

Another limitation is the difficulty in reprogramming or reconfiguring robotic systems. Changing the system's programming or hardware configuration can be time-consuming and may require specialized expertise. This can limit the system's ability to adapt to changing conditions or perform new tasks.

In recent years, advances in machine learning and artificial intelligence have helped address some of these limitations. These technologies can enable robots to learn and adapt to new tasks and environments, reducing the need for complex programming and hardware reconfiguration.
